ABSTRACT
Lamb wave scattering provides a basis for extracting meaningful information from wave propagation in plates 
and offers essential theoretical support for nondestructive evaluation and structural health monitoring. However, 
scattering analysis remains challenging due to inherent dispersion characteristics and high sensitivity to geo -
metric variations. Conventional numerical methods become computationally expensive for large spatial or high 
frequency domains. Physics informed neural networks sometimes suffer from slow convergence and unstable 
optimization when modeling high frequency or strongly heterogeneous wavefields. Fourier neural operators 
(FNOs) may suppress high frequency components that are essential for scattering analysis. To address these 
limitations, this paper proposes a dual-stage FNO framework with physics embedded and residual refinement for 
Lamb wave scattering modeling. The physics embedded stage establishes a globally consistent mapping between 
thickness distributions and scattering wavefields to capture the dominant propagation behavior, while the re -
sidual refinement stage learns discrepancies between predicted and reference wavefields to enhance the repre -
sentation of scattering characteristics. The proposed framework is validated through numerical simulations with 
random thickness defects and laser scanning vibration measurement experiments. Results demonstrate that the 
proposed approach improves global prediction accuracy and physical consistency while yielding pronounced 
error reduction in defect regions, demonstrating its effectiveness for Lamb wave scattering analysis.
1. Introduction
Guided wave scattering refers to the alteration of wave propagation 
caused by discontinuities such as geometric irregularities, thickness 
variations, or material inhomogeneities, ultimately modifying the 
propagation path, energy distribution, and dispersion characteristics. It 
has long attracted widespread attention due to the inherent involvement 
of boundary conditions and mode conversion in scattering. Lamb waves 
represent the most typical modes propagating in plate-like structures. 
Although classical, Lamb wave scattering remains the cornerstone for 
understanding more complex situations involving irregular scatterers, 
multimodal interactions and intricate boundary conditions. Accurate 
modeling and interpretation of Lamb wave scattering not only provide 
crucial insights into wave interactions but also play a vital role in 
advancing nondestructive testing (NDT) and structural health moni -
toring (SHM) technologies for ensuring the integrity and reliability of 
engineering structures.
In recent decades, extensive analytical efforts have been devoted to 
understanding guided wave scattering in plate structures. Achenbach 
[ 1 ] [ 2 ] [ 3 ] [ 4 ] developed a unified theoretical framework for elastic 
wave dynamics in plates based on the elastodynamic reciprocity theo -
rem. By establishing modal orthogonality and employing bounded vir -
tual solutions to determine expansion coefficients, the derived 
formulation enabled an elegant and complete reconstruction of wave 
responses generated by point sources at arbitrary locations and orien -
tations. This theoretical system avoids the intricate integral transforms 
commonly seen in classical formulations and has laid a solid foundation 
for subsequent advances in plate wave analysis and ultrasonic guided 
wave NDT. Building upon this foundation, Grahn [ 5 ] and Cegla et al. [ 6 ] 
systematically investigated the scattering of fundamental Lamb modes 
by asymmetric circular blind holes. Both studies adopted the wave 
function expansion method, expressing the interior and exterior wave -
fields as complete sets of Lamb and SH modes and solving for the un -
known expansion coefficients through the enforcement of stress, 
displacement, bending moment, and shear force boundary conditions.

Grahn [ 5 ] formulated a full three dimensional (3D) elastodynamic 
model complemented by Poisson-Kirchhoff plate theory for low fre -

only imposes power conservation naturally but also improves numerical 
stability and convergence. The versatility of CMEP has been demon -
strated across a wide range of applications, from thickness steps and 
welds [ 10 ] [ 11 ], to horizontal cracks and disbonds [ 9 ], and further to 
stiffened plates containing root cracks [ 12 ]. These studies underscore 
the strength of frameworks based on modal expansion in addressing 
guided wave scattering problems. Despite their rigor and physical 
transparency, these analytical and semi-analytical approaches rely on 
precise mathematical descriptions of governing equations and boundary 
conditions, involving extensive derivations and large matrix systems. 
More importantly, their effectiveness fundamentally depends on the 
ability to parameterize defect geometries in a manner compatible with 
modal expansions. Therefore, current analytical and semi-analytical 
frameworks are primarily limited to regular or parameterizable scat -
terers. Extending these powerful techniques to realistic defects with 
arbitrary geometric complexity, while easing the theoretical and 
computational burdens, remains an important challenge and a key di -
rection for future research.
In parallel with analytical developments, numerical simulation has 
also become an indispensable tool for studying guided wave scattering. 
Despite differences in implementation, numerical methods fundamen -
tally operate by discretizing the governing elastodynamic equations, 
which allows the originally continuous scattering problem to be recast 
as a finite dimensional system. Finite difference schemes employ 
structured grids to approximate spatial and temporal derivatives [ 13 ], 
whereas finite element methods discretize the waveguide into triangular 
or quadrilateral elements to accommodate complex geometries [ 14 ] 
[ 15 ]. Boundary element methods reduce the dimensionality by dis -
cretizing the structural boundary, yet still rely on numerical quadrature 
and dense boundary matrices [ 16 ]. Semi-analytical finite element for -
mulations offer a more compact alternative by exploiting periodicity or 
geometric invariance, thereby further reducing the number of degrees of 
freedom [ 17 ]. Additional hybrid strategies [ 18 ] [ 19 ], spectral element 
approaches [ 20 ] and discontinuous Galerkin variants [ 21 ], continue this 
development by combining localized discretization with higher order 
basis functions or enhanced boundary treatments. These advances 
reflect a clear trajectory in numerical scattering analysis, where 
dimensionality reduction or discretization is used to reduce computa -
tional costs, and more advanced radiation conditions are adopted to 
better represent unbounded domains. While numerical methods provide 
the flexibility to accommodate arbitrary geometries and heterogeneous 
material distributions, they inherently face trade-offs among accuracy, 
stability, and computational efficiency. High frequency Lamb wave 
simulations demand extremely fine spatial discretization (often 
exceeding ten grid points per minimum wavelength), causing the 
computational burden to increase rapidly with excitation frequency, 
domain size, and defect complexity [ 22 ] [ 23 ]. Therefore, high fidelity 
scattering simulations remain computationally expensive, particularly 
in inversion or imaging tasks that require large parametric sweeps or 
repeated forward solves. These limitations motivate the exploration of 
data-driven and physics informed learning frameworks that aim to 
preserve the fidelity of numerical solvers while mitigating their 
computational bottlenecks.
In recent years, neural networks have emerged as powerful, nearly 
universal function approximators capable of addressing a wide range of 
engineering problems. Despite their impressive performance, concerns 
regarding interpretability and physical consistency remain. Since wave 
propagation is essentially governed by partial differential equations 
(PDEs), an important research attempts to directly embed physical laws 
into the learning process. Physics informed neural networks (PINNs) 
follow this paradigm by incorporating PDE residuals, boundary condi -
tions, and initial conditions into the loss function, enabling the network 
to learn mappings that remain consistent with the underlying physics 
[ 24 ] [ 25 ] [ 26 ]. PINNs have since been applied to various wavefield 
problems, including acoustic wave simulation [ 22 ] [ 27 ], elastic wave 
inversion [ 28 ] [ 29 ], and ultrasonic imaging [ 30 ] [ 31 ], demonstrating 
their ability to recover continuous fields from sparse data and to alle -
viate the dependency on large training datasets. However, their reliance 
on high order automatic differentiation and complex multi-term residual 
losses often leads to slow convergence and unstable optimization, 
especially when modeling high frequency, dispersive, or strongly het -
erogeneous wavefields. Therefore, PINNs are typically well suited for 
solving specific modeling problems, but struggle to generalize across 
many input configurations or to efficiently approximate families of PDE 
solutions [ 32 ] [ 33 ].
This limitation has motivated the idea of neural operator learning, 
which seeks to approximate mappings between infinite dimensional 
function spaces rather than solutions to specific PDE instances. Repre -
sentative frameworks include DeepONet [ 34 ] [ 35 ] [ 36 ] and Fourier 
neural operator (FNO) [ 37 ]. Unlike PINNs, which solve a particular PDE 
for a given input, neural operators are designed to learn a family of PDE 
solutions parameterized by varying coefficients, source terms, or 
boundary geometry. DeepONet employs a branch-trunk architecture 
that separately encodes functional inputs and spatial coordinates, 
making it conceptually flexible but computationally expensive when 
dense spatiotemporal sampling is required. Its pointwise evaluation 
mechanism also makes capturing long range wave propagation or 
multi-modal interactions more challenging. FNO, in contrast, performs 
global convolution in the spectral domain through fast Fourier transform 
(FFT), enabling efficient capture and generalization of the dependency 
relationships in wavefield propagation [ 38 ] [ 39 ] [ 40 ]. This strategy may 
be particularly attractive for guided wave problems, where wave prop -
agate over large distances and accompany with dispersion, mode con -
version, and scattering. The ability of FNO to model global interactions 
with relatively few spectral modes provides a clear advantage over ar -
chitectures that rely solely on localized convolutions or pointwise 
sampling. However, the implementation of FNO involves truncating 
high frequency Fourier modes during spectral processing, which may 
suppress the high frequency and defect sensitive components crucial for 
accurate Lamb wave scattering analysis [ 41 ]. In addition, there are some 
surrogate models based on PINN and neural operators that have been 
successfully applied in different fields. However, some methods [ 42 ] 
[ 43 ] [ 44 ] mainly focus on improving the PDE consistency of global so -
lutions, while others [ 45 ] [ 46 ] [ 47 ] aim to improve prediction perfor -
mance through feature fusion or architecture coupling. These methods 
have not yet specifically solved the limitations caused by FNO spectrum 
truncation, nor have they considered the multi-scale characteristics of 
guided wave propagation and scattering. These considerations highlight 
the need to adapt and enhance neural operator frameworks when 
applying them to guided wave scattering problems. In particular, 
combining FNO with physical constraints and a residual learning strat -
egy provides a pathway to retain its strengths in capturing global wave

To address these challenges and extend the applicability of neural 
operator techniques to guided wave scattering, this study enhances the 
FNO framework by embedding the governing equation directly into the 
training process as physics constraints, followed by incorporating a re -
sidual refinement stage that models the discrepancy between pre -
dictions and reference wavefields. A finite difference scheme is 
employed to solve the Kirchhoff-Love plate equation and generate the 
out-of-plane displacement wavefields for dataset construction. The PDE 
embedding constrains the learned operator to remain consistent with 
physical laws and enhances its ability to represent global wave propa -
gation behavior, while the residual refinement stage improves the rep -
resentation of localized scattering characteristics that are often 
attenuated during spectral truncation in standard FNO architectures. 
The performance enhancement of the proposed dual-stage FNO is 
quantitatively evaluated by comparing wavefield predictions obtained 
with and without physics embedded and residual refinement. Additional 
validation is provided through laser scanning vibration measurements 
performed on an aluminum plate containing a randomly generated 
defect. Both numerical simulations and experimental observations 
demonstrate that the dual-stage FNO improves the accuracy and phys -
ical consistency of the predictions, thereby strengthening the operator 
learning framework for Lamb wave scattering. This approach also offers 
a promising direction for extending neural operator techniques to more 
complex structural configurations and practical nondestructive 
evaluation.

thickness to its corresponding wavefield, a FNO network was developed.

The first convolutional layer (Conv layer 1) uses two identical 3 × 3 
convolutions and a Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation to transform 
a single channel input thickness map into a multi-channel feature rep -
resentation. This step expands the feature dimension and extracts 
essential spatial patterns from the geometric input, providing a rich 
representation for the subsequent spectral learning stage.
The FNO layer containing two identical FNO blocks operates in fre -
quency domain to model long range interactions. Key hyperparameters 
include a channel width of 64 for FNO blocks and a Fourier mode 
truncation of (32, 32) in frequency domain. Each block applies a 2D FFT

only the first (32, 32) low frequency Fourier modes to reduce compu -
tational costs. The retained spectral coefficients are multiplied by a 
learnable complex valued matrix R , which acts as a trainable filter in 
frequency domain. The transformed features are then mapped back to

The next UFNO layer consists of a symmetric encoder-decoder

of FNO with the multi-scale feature extraction of UNet. As shown in 
Fig. 1 (b), in each encoding stage, an FNO block first extracts global in -
formation at the current scale, followed by a 3 × 3 convolution for 
downsampling, accompanied by a ReLU nonlinearity. The output of 
each encoder stage is simultaneously passed through a 1 × 1 convolution 
to generate a skip-connection tensor that preserves fine scale details for 
later fusion. Consequently, the spatial resolution of the feature maps 
gradually decreases, allowing the network to accumulate both global 
and local features in a compact latent space. In the decoding path, the 
process is reversed through four corresponding stages of upsampling. 
Each decoder stage starts with a transpose convolution to double the 
spatial resolution, followed by a ReLU activation and another FNO block 
that reconstructs the global dependencies lost during downsampling. At 
each resolution, the upsampled features are added to the corresponding 
skip-connection tensor from the encoder to fuse multi-scale information. 
Because the combination of convolution and transpose convolution 
operations may lead to one pixel misalignment between the encoder and 
decoder feature maps, a bilinear interpolation is applied to ensure per -
fect spatial alignment before the fusion. This design allows UFNO to 
account for long range wave propagation while preserving localized 
spatial variations, achieving accurate reconstruction of scattering 
wavefield.

3. Numerical simulations and results
3.1. Dataset construction
The dataset used in this study is generated through modeling of Lamb 
wave propagation in plate structures containing random defects. Fig. 2
shows the wave propagation model and the group velocity dispersion 
curve of this model. The computational domain corresponds to an

5. Discussion
5.1. Key features and advantages
The proposed dual-stage Lamb wave scattering modeling framework 
with physics embedded and residual refinement integrates the comple -
mentary strengths of PINN and FNO. By learning a direct operator 
mapping from structural parameters to full spatiotemporal scattering 
wavefields, the framework avoids the configuration specific nature and 
pointwise coordinate dependence commonly associated with PINN 
based approaches. It demonstrates the ability to generalize over a family 
of related governing problems, as reflected in its stable performance 
across scattering problems involving randomly varying defect locations, 
geometries, and thickness distributions. Meanwhile, embedding gov -
erning equations into the training process guides the learning toward 
physically consistent solutions, improving robustness and interpret -
ability compared with purely data-driven FNO models. A key advantage 
of the proposed strategy lies in residual refinement, which mitigates the 
loss of high frequency and localized scattering information arising from 
truncated spectral representations in standard FNO. The proposed 
framework enhances the representation of localized scattering features 
and sharp spatial variations, leading to improved overall prediction fi -
delity for Lamb wave scattering fields. The benefits of this dual-stage 
learning strategy are clearly demonstrated by the results shown in

Another appealing feature of the framework is its conceptual 
extensibility. Forward scattering problems and the corresponding in -
verse problems are generally governed by the similar underlying phys -
ical equations. Therefore, the proposed dual-stage strategy can be 
naturally extended to inverse scattering and defect imaging tasks, where 
defect characteristics are inferred from measured wavefields. From this 
perspective, the present framework offers a common foundation for 
forward modeling and inverse analysis. In addition, beyond Lamb wave 
scattering in plates, the proposed method is inherently general and can 
be adapted to other guided wave problems by modifying the governing 
equations and input parameterization. This flexibility makes the 
approach a promising candidate for a wide range of guided wave NDT 
and SHM applications.
It is worth noting that, the proposed method is not intended to 
replace conventional numerical approaches such as finite difference or 
finite element methods, which remain highly reliable and flexible tools 
especially when the damage geometry is known in advance and only a 
limited number of forward simulations are required. Instead, the pro -
posed framework serves as a complementary tool that offers advantages 
in scenarios demanding rapid, repeated forward predictions. Its primary 
value lies in enabling fast evaluation of multiple defect configurations, 
rather than outperforming traditional solvers in all use cases. After 
offline training, the proposed surrogate model facilitates rapid and 
repeated predictions, making it particularly attractive in the context of 
real-time or near-real-time wavefield computation. In such settings, 
surrogate approaches may become more competitive than repeated 
conventional simulations. This capability underscores the growing in -
terest in learning-based alternatives in recent years and highlights the 
relevance of the proposed direction.
5.2. Limitations and future perspectives
Despite its advantages, the present study also has several limitations 
that warrant further investigation. First, the current implementation 
focuses on a single mode and employs a simplified governing equation 
for plate waves. This study mainly focuses on the out-of-plane 
displacement field, based on the Kirchhoff-Love plate theory, which is 
applicable to simulating the A0 mode and does not explicitly explain the 
transformation of A0-S0 mode. In fact, under the considered frequency- 
thickness conditions, the response is still dominated by the A0 mode, 
while the contribution of mode conversion S0 in the out-of-plane field is 
relatively limited. Additionally, the proposed dual-stage learning strat -
egy is not restricted to the Kirchhoff-Love formulation itself. For more 
general scenarios involving multiple modes and mode conversions, it 
would be preferable to incorporate more comprehensive governing 
equations, such as the Navier elastodynamic equations. Extending the 
framework to the quantitative analysis of multimodal Lamb wave 
propagation and scattering, as well as to 3D wavefields, represents an

6. Summary
This work presents a dual-stage FNO framework with physics 
embedded and residual refinement for solving Lamb wave scattering in 
plate structures. The embedding of governing equation improves the 
physical consistency of predicted wavefields and strengthens the 
coherence of the global scattering representation. Residual refinement 
further compensates for information loss arising in FNO learning, 
particularly in wavefield components sensitive to defects and high fre -
quencies. Through this dual-stage design, the proposed framework 
achieves improved global wavefield prediction performance together 
with more accurate reconstruction of local scattering details, providing a 
more reliably description for Lamb wave scattering phenomenon. Nu -
merical simulations demonstrate that the proposed framework improves 
wavefield prediction performance, with pronounced error reduction in 
regions where conventional FNO models typically lose accuracy. The 
prediction error has achieved an overall reduction, with a performance 
improvement of approximately 30% to 45%. The scanning experiment 
further shows that the predicted wavefield energy distributions closely 
match the reference solution and measurement results, effectively 
capturing the scattering behavior. These results provide good evidence 
for the effectiveness of the proposed dual-stage FNO strategy and 
establish it as a promising modeling paradigm for guided wave scat -
tering problems. The proposed framework has the potential to be 
extended to more complex and realistic engineering scenarios, involving 
multimodal wave propagation and heterogeneous structures, providing 
a flexible foundation for future research in advanced guided wave NDT.
